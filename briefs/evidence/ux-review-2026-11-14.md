# Civic Lens production UX and visual review

Date: 2026-11-14. Site release seen: 1096 (search index: 1081). Reviewer: an independent adversarial reviewer (one browser, one context). Viewports 320, 360, 390, 1280, 1440, light and dark. Screenshots stay on the owner's machine (scratchpad ux-review folder); names are cited for reference.
Not checked by eye: glossary, about, method, vote-kinds and the full board list (text and metrics only). Reading grades are script estimates.

Verified by the lead after the review: P1-1 (H.R. 8205 lede "Introduced 9 Jul 2026" against the CRS line "introduced on April 6, 2026"), P1-2 (bills.html?status=inCommittee and passedHouse answer 503 after 2.1 to 2.2 s), P1-6 search release (search answers release 1081; the cause is the search ingest failing on a statement timeout in TypedEntryReader.ReadOfficialsAsync, 6.1 s on production).

## Overall verdict

The site is fast, stable and visually coherent, and the ZIP flow on the home page is good. It is not ready to launch as a trust product. Bill pages print a wrong "Introduced" date that contradicts the other dates on the same page. Three of the five bill status filters return a raw JSON 503 error. The AI summary on vote pages, the loudest block on the page, restates the title and adds nothing, while the text that says what the bill does sits at the bottom and is cut mid-sentence. Vote pages do not show how your saved members voted, although the home page promises it and the approved mockup has that section. The House Republican "broke from their party most" slot on the home page goes to the Guam delegate, whose base is 51 split votes against about 250 for other members. Lists repeat long AI labels on every card, the bills list sorts by a key no reader cares about, and Senate vote pages have no party counts. Craft about 75 percent, content logic about 55 percent. Nielsen heuristics 22 of 40 (status 3, real-world match 2, user control 2, consistency 2, error prevention 3, recognition 2, efficiency 2, minimalist design 2, error recovery 1, help 3).

## P1: harms trust or blocks a task

P1-1. Bill pages show a wrong "Introduced" date. H.R. 8205: lede and timeline say "Introduced 9 Jul 2026"; the AI summary says "introduced on April 6, 2026"; cosponsors "Added on 9 Jul 2026: Ken Calvert", then "Added later: Robert Aderholt (23 Apr 2026), Jake Auchincloss (9 Apr 2026)". H.R. 5994 says "Introduced 12 Nov 2026" while its latest action is 10 Nov 2025. The page seems to print the latest sponsorship date (the action revision event_on) as the introduction date; the "1 on the day it was introduced and 102 who joined later" count is wrong for the same reason. Fix: print the introduction date; test that no cosponsor date is before it.

P1-2. bills.html?status=inCommittee, passedHouse and other return a raw JSON 503 after about 2.1 s. passedSenate 1.75 s, issue=firearms 2.0 s, plain list 1.5 to 1.7 s. The reader sees unstyled JSON. Fix: fast status query; a styled error page with a way back for every 5xx.

P1-3. The vote AI summary is filler and the useful text is buried and cut. Title: "House passed H.R. 1013, the Retirement Fairness for Charities and Educational Institutions Act of 2025, on final passage." AI summary (82 words): "The House voted on final passage of H.R. 1013, a bill titled ... The question was whether the bill should pass, and the measure was approved ... Its title points to retirement plans ..., but the vote itself only shows that the House approved the bill as a whole." Senate example ends "The vote concerned a nomination rather than a bill, so no legislation is named. The record does not list any policy issues for this vote." What the bill does sits in "The bill" card at the page bottom, cut at "which are a…". Fix: one sentence on what the bill does at the top; drop the AI vote summary when it adds nothing beyond title, result and counts; never cut mid-sentence.

P1-4. Vote pages do not show how your members voted. With a saved ZIP the page shows a "Your members" strip with names only. Home says "Enter your ZIP code to see how your members voted on this." The mockup has "Your members on this vote"; bill and issue pages have it. The member finder is a closed fold about 6,000 px down on mobile and needs "Apply filters" (reload). Fix: "Your members on this vote" under the result; a finder that filters while typing.

P1-5. The home page ranks a delegate as the top House Republican party breaker: "James Moylan, 26 of 51 split votes"; second is Kimberlyn King-Hinds, 23 of 49; others have about 250. His page says "Republican representative for Guam, district 0". Fix: list delegates apart or set a base floor; say "Delegate for Guam"; never print "district 0".

P1-6. Search snippets leak raw source data ("Member: B001307, James Baird, R, IN, Not Voting Member: B001309, Tim Burchett", "voting 8 Source data: https://clerk.house.gov/evs/2026/roll189.xml Bill: 119 hr 1041 Title:"); some start mid-sentence. Search says "Release 1081" while the site says 1096. No total count, only "20 results on this page". Fix: snippets from the published summary only; show the total; keep release numbers equal or hide them.

P1-7. A "Corrected record" banner with no explanation sits on 4 of 12 sampled records: "Civic Lens corrected this bill or resolution on 14 Nov 2026, 12:02 UTC." It does not say what changed; dates of today suggest a routine refresh. Fix: banner only for a real correction, with what changed; a routine refresh says "Updated" or nothing.

P1-8. Senate vote pages have no party counts: "This record does not show party counts." No break list. The site holds each senator's party and vote and the Senate boards use them. Fix: Senate party bars from the member list.

## P2: clear quality problems

P2-1. The member record page (454 votes, 25 a page) has only "Next page". Fix: issue, bill number, against party, Yes or No filters.
P2-2. "How X voted" squares: text says "Each square is one vote" but the strip shows only breaks and misses, so 12 identical hatched squares; the mockup shows latest roll calls as Yes or No. Squares about 28 px with no visible label. Fix: mockup behavior or cut.
P2-3. The bills list sorts by latest sponsorship; page 1 is 25 "In committee" bills. Fix: default to latest official action, passed and enacted first.
P2-4. Code-written bill titles carry no subject: "S. 5718: introduced by Lisa Blunt Rochester", then "Sponsor: Lisa Blunt Rochester". Fix: official or short title in lists and search.
P2-5. Six AI label wordings are live: "An AI summary of the official record"; "AI summary of the bill text. An AI model wrote this. Each sentence comes with an exact quote from the text. Code checked the quotes, not the meaning of the sentences." (29 words, on every card, and a four-line all-caps pill on the bill page); "An AI model summarized the document text. Code checks each quote."; "An AI summary of this press release."; "Drafted by an AI model from this source. A person checked it before publication."; "Written automatically from Congress.gov data, with no AI". Tense differs; some end with a period; in lists the label is purple like a link but is not a link; a 25-card list shows it 25 times. Fix: one short chip ("AI summary" or "No AI") with the detail in a popover; long text on the record page only.
P2-6. "An AI summary of the official record" sits above CRS text that is not AI; one excerpt is CRS boilerplate ("The following analysis is based on draft bill text ..."). Fix: skip boilerplate first sentences; place each label next to the text it covers.
P2-7. The AI summary of a CRS summary (H.R. 8205) is 219 words at about grade 17, above the limit of 14. The page shows a cut CRS excerpt, the rewrite, and the CRS fold: three versions of one text. Fix: a two-sentence plain summary and the CRS fold.
P2-8. Unclear numbers: "311 records in this release" under one executive order title (the four counts add to 693, all executive actions); "Half of Yes and No votes: 105.5" on every bar; bare "63%" chips; "94 of 255 split votes cast with most House Democrats" on the official page against "161 of 255 against" on the board; "25 records other than votes"; "Release 1096." in list captions; "19627", "1592" without separators next to "1,100"; "1 Republicans", "1 Senate members"; issue tiles "0 substantive votes" with "119th Congress" 16 times.
P2-9. Navigation: the menu lacks Statements, Search, Glossary, Weekly briefs and Home; "Compare parties" opens "Compare the parties" at boards.html while compare.html "Compare your members" repeats the home ZIP form; footer "How we check records" opens "How the counts work"; briefs.html is live and empty; extensionless URLs (/officials, /votes, /search, /officials/henry-cuellar) and legacy /web/records/{id}.html answer 404 with no redirect; record URLs are 64-character hashes.
P2-10. A saved ZIP adds two "Your members" bars (header and strip), each with "Forget"; about 230 px before the breadcrumb at 360 px.
P2-11. ZIP results put weak content first: ten issue rows "0 bills sponsored, 49 substantive votes cast" per card before "How your House members voted"; the featured vote still says "Enter your ZIP code ..." after lookup; "Compare your members" link is nearly invisible.
P2-12. Vote list titles have no house style ("House passes...", "House Approves Resolution ...", "Senate voted to end debate ... .", "... and the motion passed."); "a nominee" against a named person; a Spanish statement headline; results "Confirmed (Nomination Confirmed)", "Passed (Bill Passed)", "Failed (Amendment Rejected)".
P2-13. Lists have no result count and no active filter chip; the filter fold stays open after apply; paging is "Next page" only; officials list sorts by first name, 22 pages, no photos; executive actions have no issue filter.
P2-14. Long mobile pages: home 8,453 px, vote 8,629 px (83 names open), glossary 18,551 px, full board 25,858 px; H.R. 8205 prints 103 cosponsors with dates as one paragraph. Fix: fold long member lists.
P2-15. The embedded vote map colors Yes or No only; "By party" only on the map page; a 392 to 18 vote is a dark blob; the mobile tile view takes about 2,500 px.
P2-16. Statements are thin and stale: 32 statements, newest 31 Oct; "Date not stated" rows; a quoted text captioned "Not in quotation marks in the source."
P2-17. The About page says "Civic Lens does not summarize these documents yet" while executive order pages carry an AI summary.
P2-18. Tap targets: vote page 150 of 174 under 44 px (141 under 24 px); full board 227 of 244 under 44 px; inline links 15 to 19 px tall; the "Try again" button after a failed ZIP lookup looks disabled.

## P3: polish

The same checkbox icon on every vote card, also failed votes. Three link styles for record titles. Three type families (Archivo, Gabarito, Figtree); body 15 px, meta 12 to 13 px; raise body to 16 px on mobile. Desktop hero leaves about 550 px of empty navy; featured result below the fold at 1280x800. Home executive action cards touch and indent unlike vote cards. Stat tiles stack full width on mobile (mockup 2x2). Vote H1 runs 7 lines at 360 px; cap AI titles at about 70 characters. Glossary term button inside an H2 on home. "What this vote meant" is boilerplate. "Key facts" on executive pages repeats the lede; "Checked by code. Revision 2." is internal language. Bills intro repeats itself. "Congress 119" against "119th Congress". "Votes on this bill" on a law shows only a Senate gap note. "Sponsored by Mike Quigley" not a link. Search H1 smaller than other H1s; two search boxes. Mobile menu sheet has no scrim. Joe Biden in the Executive filter with no "former" mark. Footer has four links. No print stylesheet, no forced-colors rules.

## Answers to the owner's questions

1. Visual quality: consistent and clean in both themes; dark mode well made. No horizontal overflow at any width; layout shift 0.00; largest paint under 0.9 s. Server time 70 to 350 ms on most pages, 0.5 to 1.0 s on records, officials and boards, 1.4 to 1.8 s on bills and issue pages. Weak points are density and rhythm: same-weight cards, small grey meta, long all-caps pills, 8,000 to 25,000 px phone pages. Manual contrast failures: the ghost "Try again" button and the "Compare your members" link.
2. Navigation: breadcrumbs correct everywhere; vote to member and bill links work. "How did my representative vote on X?": recent vote 1 action from home after ZIP; any other vote 6 actions and a long scroll, or up to 19 record pages. This core task is the weakest path. "What does this bill do and who backs it?": 2 taps. "Who breaks with their party most?": 2 taps. "What did the President sign this month?": 2 taps, titles only.
3. Information: best are the ZIP vote comparison, party boards, cosponsor party bar, bill timeline, executive order summaries. Cut: filler AI vote summaries, "What this vote meant", "Key facts", ZIP card issue bullets, compare.html as a separate page, Briefs until a brief exists, repeated "119th Congress", checkbox icons. Merge: CRS excerpt plus AI rewrite plus CRS fold; the two "Your members" bars. Promote: what the bill does on vote pages, "Your members on this vote", party bars above the map, bills that moved, "By party" on the embedded map. Demote: mobile tile map, the 83-name break list, "Latest sponsorship" dates, release numbers.
4. AI labels and text: not consistent (six wordings, two visual forms, labels over non-AI text). Vote summaries reword title and result; the CRS-based bill summary rewords CRS at equal length. Bill-text and executive order summaries add information, but quote fragments are often empty. Reading level: page explainers grade 4 to 6 (choppy, below the 10 to 14 band), vote summaries 10, bill text and statement summaries 12, executive summary 14, CRS rewrite 17.
5. Numbers: see P2-8. Add one plain sentence by the first split-vote figure: "On votes where the two parties took opposite sides, he voted with the Republicans 161 times out of 255."
6. Accessibility: good skip link, lang, one H1, no level skips, labelled landmarks, chart text alternatives and tables, alt text, 2 px focus rings, reduced motion. Problems: tap targets, outline none on header search and ZIP inputs (check by hand), popover inside a heading, 28 px squares, purple non-link label text, 12 to 13 px meta, no forced colors.
7. Retention: today a reference site used once. Readers would return weekly for "this week for your three members" on home, a real weekly brief, and email or RSS of it. Party boards and "who broke ranks this week" are the most shareable.

## What earns praise

Speed and stability (no layout shift, no overflow, no console errors). ZIP lookup handles a ZIP that spans two districts and says so; specific error text. The vote comparison for a reader's members with "Same vote" chips. Every chart has a legend, a text alternative and a table; hatching backs up color. Dark theme and the three-way theme control. Short, correct honesty notes. The bill number match card and the member card in search.
