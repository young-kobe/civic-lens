# Phase 2. Graph and statistical research

**Recommendation.** Model typed relationships now. Keep the authoritative evidence and review decisions in PostgreSQL. Use a disposable graph built from a single published release to explore questions with many paths. The [two-bill experiment](experiments/published-graph.md) found one useful shared backer and showed why a dense network can overstate the evidence.

## Which questions need a graph?

| Reader question | Proposed paths and methods | What must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| Who spoke about this bill and then acted? | Start with the existing approved statement, relationship and vote or action revisions. Use SQL for a known path. Add explicit cross-speaker links only after reviewing the attribution. | Quote, speaker, dated cast or sponsorship, vote question, and bill or amendment version. A motion to table changes the interpretation. |
| Who explicitly backed or challenged a president's claim? | Link claim revision to a legislator's own response and its quotation. Display their subsequent action on the exact measure alongside it. Define a cohort in advance, such as chamber leadership on the claim date. | Keep the member's expressed position separate from a cast, party identity or silence. Show coverage and unobserved responses. |
| Which commitments progressed? | Connect the directive to a named agency, deadline, implementation record and outcome series. A missing step narrows the next search. Add spending or judicial records when identifiers establish scope. | Distinguish an order, appropriation, obligation, payment, injunction and measured outcome. Track subsequent legal status. [Congress API](https://api.congress.gov/), [USAspending data sources](https://www.usaspending.gov/data-sources) and [CourtListener search](https://wiki.free.law/c/courtlistener/help/api/rest/v4/search). |
| Which coalitions recur on this issue? | Compare co-sponsorship and voting separately, within a chamber, time window and motion type. Show shared bills or eligible votes and denominators. | Unanimous bills generate many member pairs. Compare against bill popularity and member activity. Retain record-level drill-down and missing casts. |

Use nodes for identifiable, versioned evidence and edges for a specific predicate and direction. Keep event time, knowledge cutoff, source family and approval on the relevant facts. A derived edge must retain its contributing record revisions. A general-purpose triple table makes foreign keys and domain-specific evidence checks harder. The existing relationship revision and vote-bill link give simpler precedents. PROV-O supplies useful terms for derivation and attribution if we later need interchange. [Relationship reference](../architecture/statements.md#relationship-drafting) and [W3C provenance vocabulary](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/).

## A local graph in relationship review

A reviewer opens one proposed relationship. The default view shows its two endpoints, the decision in context, and the cited passages. Selecting a node opens its exact source. Selecting a link shows the predicate, direction and evidence on both sides. A dashed link marked “may support” is a question for the reviewer. Solid named links, such as “part of” and “recorded in,” identify documentary relationships. Start from one question, hold the layout still while it is inspected, and reveal nearby records on request. This borrows the local neighborhood from [Obsidian's local graph](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/graph#Local%20graph).

**Try it locally.** Open the [review prototype](experiments/review-graph/review.html) from a checkout. On the removal-deadline case, compare Rep. Dean's statement with the Senate-passed bill. Section 3 starts the clock upon a valid request. Expand the member cast to see what Dean voted on. Switch to the victim-impact case. The same provision and vote are available, but a claim about outcomes calls for evidence about people's experiences. The speaker in the second case is Sen. Cruz. The House cast still belongs to Rep. Dean. Accept, reject or mark the proposed link insufficient and give a reason. Drafts survive browsing within the tab. Reloading clears practice decisions. The prototype uses [frozen source inputs](experiments/evidence-trials/sources.json).

**Production boundary.** Project a small neighborhood from typed PostgreSQL joins. Each node carries a domain kind, stable ID, exact revision, display label and source locator. Each edge carries its predicate, direction, endpoint revisions and evidence. Keep the local layout separate from the facts. Fetch the selected relationship with its endpoints first. Expand one hop by an allowed predicate with a bounded page size and explicit remaining results. Deduplicate nodes and edges by identity and retain multiple distinct relationships between a pair. A missing or withdrawn dependency must be represented in the review state. Loading another neighborhood must preserve the draft on its original relationship. Reuse the authenticated Host's existing version-checked decision handlers. A stale submission reloads the changed evidence for review before a new decision can be saved. The prototype uses a fixed layout and in-memory fixtures. Durable decisions, access checks, graph pagination and revision conflicts belong to implementation. [Relationship review](../architecture/statements.md#relationship-drafting) and [evidence interface](evidence-interface.md).

**Public reuse.** The [reader prototype](experiments/review-graph/reader.html) loads the same graph, evidence inspector and list renderer from its own public-source example. It receives the bill, provision, roll call and member cast. The review fixture, unassessed claim links and reviewer notes are absent from its payload. In production, PublicApi builds a separate projection from eligible revisions in a single published release. Enforce eligibility before sending nodes, edges, totals or expansion hints. Recheck corrections and withdrawals across both map and list, using the [public route trial](delivery.md#acceptance-trial). The examples illustrate the boundary using primary sources. They make no claim that Civic Lens has published either proposed relationship.

**Obsidian integration.** In the reader, select **Export to Obsidian**. Copy the downloaded `.canvas` file into an Obsidian vault and open it with the built-in Canvas plugin. It contains all four public example records, even when the member cast is collapsed on screen. Each card carries a source URL and the pinned source XML digest. The three directed links retain their documentary predicates. A downloaded file represents the example at export time. A later correction cannot update a copy in someone's vault. The browser replay inspects the downloaded JSON. Opening the file in Obsidian desktop remains a manual compatibility check. [Obsidian Canvas](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/canvas) and [JSON Canvas 1.0](https://jsoncanvas.org/spec/1.0/).

Juggl is an optional community plugin for analysts who already maintain notes and typed links in an Obsidian vault. This trial exports text cards. A vault-backed note workflow would need its own mapping for stable note IDs, links and source revisions. Keep the proposed relationship, both cited passages and the decision together in the native Civic Lens review flow. If neighborhood size later makes layout or navigation difficult, compare a web renderer such as Cytoscape.js with the existing map and list on the same tasks before selecting a dependency. [Juggl](https://github.com/HEmile/juggl) and [Cytoscape.js](https://js.cytoscape.org/).

**Decision test.** Give reviewers equivalent unfamiliar cases in map and list formats, alternating the order. Ask them to identify the exact motion and member, find the qualifying passage, and handle a related outcome claim with insufficient evidence. Compare unsupported acceptances, missed sufficient links, source-finding time and successful keyboard completion at the same review budget. If the map adds time or mistakes, keep the evidence inspector and list as the default and make the map optional. Across both layouts the reviewer should be able to finish the task without dragging, zooming or decoding color. The [browser replay](experiments/review-graph/check-view.py) serves the fixtures on loopback. It checks the map and list, search, navigation, draft recovery, case isolation, public assets and the Canvas download. From the repository root, run `python3 docs/evidence-paths/experiments/review-graph/check-view.py` with Playwright Chromium installed. A local Chromium executable can be passed as the first argument. Reviewer time and error rates remain unmeasured.

## Tool choice

| Option | Use it for | Cost or decision criterion |
|---|---|---|
| PostgreSQL joins and bounded recursive queries | Exact paths, version binding, deterministic explanations and release-consistent reads | Start here for fixed paths. Limit depth, predicates and fan-out. Check cycles and query plans against representative reads. [PostgreSQL recursive queries](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/queries-with.html). |
| NetworkX offline | Projections, common neighbors, connected components and degree sensitivity | Easy to inspect and discard. Memory use can grow with the number of member pairs. Sample first and bound the projection. [NetworkX weighted projection](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/reference/algorithms/generated/networkx.algorithms.bipartite.projection.weighted_projected_graph.html). |
| Apache AGE or a dedicated Neo4j read model | Variable-length path workloads if measured SQL becomes hard to maintain or too slow | AGE adds an extension and compatibility work. A separate store adds synchronization, recovery and withdrawal checks. Measure import, memory, query latency, freshness and rebuild time using an offline copy. [Apache AGE](https://age.apache.org/) and [Neo4j memory estimates](https://neo4j.com/docs/graph-data-science/current/common-usage/memory-estimation/). |
| Graph-assisted retrieval and GraphRAG | Entity-centered search or broad questions across many documents | Entity resolution can merge people, programs or bills incorrectly. Generated community summaries add cost and another source of error. Require exact source paths and measure benefit over text retrieval. [GraphRAG research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130) and [local search design](https://microsoft.github.io/graphrag/query/local_search/). |

The [bill-section clustering pilot](experiments/section-clusters.md) compares K-means groups with direct text neighbors. It found cross-bill matches and groups driven by amendment wording. Keep a reader's path to the source even when a cluster suggests the connection.

## Which AI or statistical study comes next?

Start with evidence selection for the private reviewer. The [experiments on existing data](experiments/applied-methods.md) also test provision clustering and bill timelines. The remaining choices each have a reader question and a condition that would justify more work.

| Reader or reviewer question | Next method and inputs | How to choose |
|---|---|---|
| Can the reviewer find enough evidence and recognize a gap? | Use exact joins and lexical search to retrieve. Try a cross-encoder that scores each quote and passage together. Compare a fixed evidence-sufficiency rule with a learned abstention rule on independently labeled families. | Keep the candidate pool and five review slots fixed for reranking. Compare evidence coverage, unsupported attributions, abstentions and review time on unseen families. Use a separate calibration set to choose a threshold. [Evidence reranking study](https://aclanthology.org/2026.finnlp-1.32/) and [selective question answering](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.503/). |
| Can we surface an overlooked policy mechanism in another bill? | Compare direct text neighbors with semantic neighbors. A reviewer labels shared mechanism, shared issue only, and drafting boilerplate. Sample both disagreements and routine pairs across bills. | Count useful distinct suggestions in the first five per anchor and missed known links. Expand issue tagging only when per-issue precision and recall transfer to new families. |
| Which disagreements should a human label next? | Compare active learning by classifier disagreement with random selection from the same development pool at equal reviewer-minute budgets. Include rare issues, non-claims and ambiguous actors. | Use the same untouched, independently sampled test set for both methods. Retain active selection only if it yields a better classifier or comparable quality in fewer reviewer minutes. [Text annotation study](https://research.google/pubs/the-practical-challenges-of-active-learning-a-case-study-from-live-experimentation/). |
| Which votes explain a split among officials on an issue? | Compare observed pairwise agreement with exploratory PCA or an ideal-point model in one chamber. Use dated casts and eligibility, account for missed votes, and hold out later motions and bill families. | Inspect the individual motions driving the result and its sensitivity to party and issue mix. Offer a comparison when it explains an observable voting pattern on unseen votes. [Voteview method](https://legacy.voteview.com/w-nominate.htm). |
| Which issue saw unusual legislative activity this week? | Count distinct substantive decisions by event week and issue. Compare a trailing baseline with a change-point detector. Replay in release order, tracking source coverage, recesses, duplicate motions and backfills. | Compare both with the date-sorted feed on later weeks. Count alerts a reviewer traces to a material development and those caused by collection changes. Keep date ordering if alerts add little. |
| How long has a comparable bill usually waited for its next step? | Compare observed cohort counts with a right-censored time-to-event estimate by chamber, Congress and stage. Preserve each denominator and source action. | Widen coverage and audit uncoded reports before reporting a typical duration. Suppress a percentile when follow-up or event counts cannot support it. [Time-to-report experiment](experiments/applied-methods.md#when-did-a-bill-receive-a-committee-report). |

For polling, the current single-question fixture exercises joins. A trend needs repeated questions with comparable wording, populations, response options and field dates. Stratify by pollster and method first. Compare any pooled estimate with the individual waves and their reported uncertainty. A separate review still decides whether a vote addressed the question that was asked. [Poll comparison](evidence-interface.md#offline-interface-check).

## Next experiments

1. Use the [cross-issue baseline](experiments/issue-mapping.md) to identify failure modes. Freeze a separate release and questions across issue families for the comparison. Include ambiguous names, absent evidence, procedural votes and evidence published after the cutoff. Annotate acceptable sources and sufficient evidence sets. Preserve questions with no gold path.
2. On the same snapshot compare exact joins plus text search, hybrid lexical and semantic retrieval, and the same retrieval plus typed paths of at most three hops. Use equal candidate and review budgets. Record sufficient evidence in the first five results, wrong-entity and after-cutoff hits, p95 latency, tokens and reviewer minutes. Inspect every case where adding a graph changes the assessment.
3. For exploratory PCA, use a member-by-roll-call matrix within one chamber and Congress. Define eligibility by term and motion type. Distinguish missing, present, Yes and No. Test alternative handling of missing observations. Compare components with pairwise agreement and established Voteview estimates. Hold out a time period and vary the issue mix. Look for stable sets of votes a reader can inspect. [Voteview cast codes](https://voteview.com/articles/data_help_votes) and [methodology](https://voteview.com/about).
4. Consider a persistent graph engine only when a named reader query exceeds the agreed latency or maintenance budget. Require identical eligible paths, exact revisions, and withdrawal behavior before comparing speed. Accept graph retrieval only if held-out evidence recall or reviewer effort improves at an acceptable false-support rate.

The present experiment establishes computational agreement on one fixed path. We have no production benchmark for a graph engine, measured GraphRAG lift, or evidence for a public ideology score.
