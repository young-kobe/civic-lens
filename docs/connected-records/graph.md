# Phase 2. Graph and statistical research

**Recommendation.** Model typed relationships now in PostgreSQL. Keep authoritative evidence and review decisions in PostgreSQL, and serve known public paths through `public_api` views using correlated `LATERAL` queries (per [search rules](../architecture/search.md)). If a named reader path ever exceeds its latency budget under valid `LATERAL` SQL, add revision-ranged projection rows to `CivicLens.PublicApi`'s search database via `ReleaseIngest` instead of adding a second database engine. Use disposable NetworkX scripts offline only for exploratory projections. The [two-bill experiment](experiments/published-graph.md) showed why projecting dense member-pair graphs overstates thin evidence (338 sponsorships produced 56,281 member pairs, 56,280 from one bill).

## Which questions need a graph?

| Reader question | Proposed paths and methods | What must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| Who spoke about this bill and then acted? | Start with existing approved statement, relationship, and vote or action revisions. Use `LATERAL` SQL over `public_api` views for known 1-to-3 hop paths. Add a "speaker sponsored or cosponsored the bill" baseline before testing semantic retrieval. | Quote, exact speaker and host release, dated cast or edition-bound sponsorship, roll-call question, motion polarity, and bill or amendment version. |
| Who explicitly backed or challenged a president's claim? | Link claim revision to a legislator's own response and quotation. Display their subsequent action on the exact measure alongside it. Define the cohort in advance (such as chamber leadership on the claim date). | Keep the member's expressed position separate from a cast, party identity, or silence. Show source coverage (R-56) and unobserved responses. |
| Which commitments progressed? | Connect the directive to a named agency, deadline, implementation record, and outcome series. Add spending (FAIN) or judicial records when identifiers establish scope. | Distinguish an order, appropriation, obligation, outlay, injunction, and measured outcome. Track legal status and reporting lags. [Congress API](https://api.congress.gov/), [USAspending data sources](https://www.usaspending.gov/data-sources), and [CourtListener search](https://wiki.free.law/c/courtlistener/help/api/rest/v4/search). |
| Which cosponsor groups recur on this issue? | Compare co-sponsorship and voting separately, within a chamber, full-session time window, and motion type. Compare against a party, chamber, committee, and degree-preserving null plus leave-one-bill-out range and next-window replication. | Unanimous and popular bills generate dense member pairs. Do not use a "coalition" or "bloc" label until every pattern gate in [pipeline rules](pipeline.md#rules-for-patterns) passes. Retain record-level drill-down and missing casts. |

Use typed tables for versioned evidence and edges for specific predicates and directions. Keep event time, knowledge cutoff, source family, and approval on the relevant facts. A derived edge retains its contributing record revisions. Do not introduce a generic triple table, because it weakens foreign keys and domain-specific evidence checks. The existing relationship revision and vote-bill link give simpler precedents. PROV-O supplies useful terms for derivation and attribution if interchange is needed later. [Relationship reference](../architecture/statements.md#relationship-drafting) and [W3C provenance vocabulary](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/).

## A local graph in relationship review

A maintainer opens one proposed relationship. The default view shows its two endpoints, the decision in context, and the cited passages. Selecting a node opens its exact source. Selecting a link shows the predicate, role, motion polarity, direction, and evidence on both sides. A dashed link marked "proposed" is a question for the maintainer. Solid named links, such as "part of" and "recorded in", identify documentary Plane 1 relationships. Hold the layout still while inspecting one question, and reveal nearby records one hop at a time on request. This borrows the local neighborhood concept from [Obsidian's local graph](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/graph#Local%20graph).

**Status of the local prototype.** The existing local prototypes ([review](experiments/review-graph/review.html) and [reader](experiments/review-graph/reader.html)) remain checked in as an early visual and interaction baseline, **superseded for production design** by the 2026-11-24 review. Do not copy four known defects from that prototype into production:
1. `review.js` mutates a recorded decision in place ("Update decision"); production decisions are immutable and append-only (`ReviewHistoryPanel` precedent).
2. `review.js` places Rep. Dean's House cast beside Sen. Cruz's victim-impact quote without labeling the different official in the edge label, and does not label third-party release hosting ("Quoted in Sen. Klobuchar's release") in the card title row.
3. `reader.html` lacks any statement node, vote-role limit line, or insufficient-evidence state, hides navigation under `<noscript>`, and says a whole-bill suspension vote "tells you where they stood".
4. `reader.html` promotes "Export to Obsidian" next to the primary CTA. Drop Obsidian Canvas export from the public reader UI (downloaded vault copies cannot be withdrawn and answer no regular-citizen question); replace it with a plain **"Copy link and citation"** control inside the Audit Drawer carrying permalink, release number, revision, and as-of date.

**Production boundary.** Project a bounded neighborhood from typed PostgreSQL `LATERAL` joins over `public_api` views. Each node carries a domain kind, stable ID, exact revision, display label, and one-step source URL. Each edge carries its predicate, target role, motion polarity, direction, endpoint revisions, and evidence. Keep layout state separate from facts. Expand one hop by allowed predicate with bounded page size and explicit remaining counts. Label any cast whose member differs from a quoted speaker explicitly with that member's name on the edge. Enforce eligibility and the release-independent withdrawal tombstone ledger before sending any node, edge, total, or expansion hint. Reuse the authenticated Host's existing immutable version-checked decision handlers (`RelationshipRevision`, `ReviewReasons.InsufficientEvidence`, and `RelationshipStaleness`).

**Decision test.** Treat the structured timeline/list as the default reader view (faster to scan, mobile-friendly, accessible) and the local neighborhood map as an optional interactive toggle. Before expanding graph UI work, compare map and list views in the three-state Evidence Path mockup and the 6-person think-aloud ([reader decisions](reader.md#decision-test)). The existing [browser replay](experiments/review-graph/check-view.py) checks the archived baseline; extend its replacement to fail on console errors and verify identical facts in map and list views.

## Tool choice

| Option | Use it for | Cost or decision criterion |
|---|---|---|
| PostgreSQL `public_api` views with correlated `LATERAL` joins | Default for all known 1-to-3 hop paths, version binding, deterministic explanations, and release-consistent reads | Start here. Always use `LATERAL` or `EXISTS` with `OFFSET 0` per [search rules](../architecture/search.md), never plain joins against `active_records` views. Note that the earlier 4-second timeout in [published-graph](experiments/published-graph.md) used a forbidden plain join and does not measure valid SQL cost. |
| Revision-ranged projection rows in `PublicApi` search DB (`ReleaseIngest`) | First scale-out option if a named reader neighborhood query exceeds latency budgets under valid `LATERAL` SQL | Reuses existing release ingest, revision-range closing on withdrawal/drop, rollback handling, and verification commands without adding a new engine. |
| NetworkX offline | Exploratory degree sensitivity, null models, and connected components on frozen exports | Easy to inspect and discard. Bound graph sizes and never serve live reads from it. |
| Apache AGE or Neo4j | Variable-length path workloads only if both `LATERAL` SQL and `ReleaseIngest` projections fail a measured latency or maintenance budget | Adds synchronization, backup, recovery, and withdrawal-tombstone enforcement overhead. Out of scope for this plan until a named query proves the need. |
| GraphRAG | Broad cross-document summaries | Entity resolution errors and generated community summaries add cost and attribution risk. Out of scope unless held-out retrieval proves lift over lexical and typed-link baselines. |

The [bill-section clustering pilot](experiments/section-clusters.md) showed weak separation (silhouette 0.030 to 0.036, mean seed ARI 0.38 to 0.41) and confirmed that ranked cross-bill neighbors outperform clusters. Do not run more clustering work.

## Which AI or statistical study comes next?

Start with evidence selection and abstention for the private Plane 2 pilot at realistic full-release corpus scale. The remaining studies each wait for a named product trigger.

| Reader or maintainer question | Next method and inputs | How to choose |
|---|---|---|
| Can the maintainer find sufficient evidence and abstain cleanly at full corpus scale? | Test BM25 plus explicit bill-vote join against a full-release distractor corpus, plus a calibrated BM25 score-threshold abstention baseline. Only if that misses coverage or abstention targets, test a cross-encoder reranker or learned abstention rule (folded into R-66). | Keep candidate pool, 5 review slots, and full-release corpus fixed. Compare coverage, unsupported attribution rate and risk, wrong-speaker count, abstentions, and maintainer review minutes on unseen families. |
| Can we surface an overlooked policy mechanism in another bill? | Compare direct lexical neighbors with semantic neighbors when provision-lineage features enter scope. | Count useful distinct suggestions in top 5 per anchor and false continuations that change legal effect on at least 29 dropped/changed transitions. |
| Which disagreements should the solo maintainer review next? | Prioritize high-salience stratified frames or classifier disagreement inside the weekly review budget (R-66), never an unbounded queue. | Compare against random selection on the same untouched holdout at equal maintainer-minute budgets. |
| Which votes explain a split among officials on an issue? | Compare observed pairwise agreement with exploratory PCA or NOMINATE in one chamber, using dated casts, eligibility, and motion polarity. | Inspect individual motions driving the split and explain agenda-control and intra-chamber scaling limits before publishing (R-69). |
| Which issue saw unusual legislative activity this week? | Single-window anomaly detection on distinct substantive decisions by event week and issue, using a trailing baseline adjusted for recesses, backfills, and duplicate procedural motions. | Replay in release order and compare against the date-sorted feed. Keep date ordering if alerts mostly reflect collection artifacts. |
| How long has a comparable bill usually waited for its next step? | Right-censored Kaplan-Meier time-to-event estimates by chamber, Congress, and stage. | Audit uncoded report actions and suppress medians or percentiles with fewer than 10 events or fewer than 20 bills still at risk. [Time-to-report experiment](experiments/applied-methods.md#when-did-a-bill-receive-a-committee-report). |

For polling (R-69), a trend needs repeated questions with comparable wording, populations, response options, and field dates, stratified by pollster and method. The maintainer still reviews whether a vote addressed the question asked. [Poll comparison](evidence-interface.md#offline-interface-check).
