The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the Consumer Price Index for All
Urban Consumers (CPI-U) monthly. CPI-U is the most-cited headline measure
of U.S. inflation; the year-over-year percentage change of CPI-U is what
mainstream coverage typically means by "the inflation rate."

The Federal Reserve's preferred inflation measure for policy purposes is
the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, published by the
Bureau of Economic Analysis. PCE typically runs slightly below CPI-U
because of methodological differences (substitution effects, scope of
goods covered).

Both series, plus historical data and revisions, are available via FRED
at https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCSL and
https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PCEPI . Use these series rather than
secondary citations when a specific period's number matters, since both
are revised after first release.
