The CDC's published position is that the routine childhood and adolescent
vaccines on its Recommended Immunization Schedule, and the seasonal influenza
and COVID-19 vaccines authorized for use in the United States, are safe and
effective for the populations they are recommended for. Common side effects
(soreness, low-grade fever, fatigue) are short-lived; serious adverse events
are rare and tracked through the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System
(VAERS) and the Vaccine Safety Datalink.

The CDC, the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, the
American Academy of Pediatrics, and the World Health Organization have
each, in published reviews, found no causal link between routine childhood
vaccines and autism. The 1998 Wakefield study that suggested such a link
was retracted by The Lancet in 2010.

This entry summarizes the published positions of these bodies. Individual
clinical decisions are between a patient and their physician.
